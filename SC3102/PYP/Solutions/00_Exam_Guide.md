## 2. Topic recipes (whole course, in lecture order)

### A. Periodicity (Signals p.9–15; L0 p.33)
- **Proof from the definition:** show $x(t+T)=x(t)$ **for all t**. For $\sum_ng(t-Tn)$, re-index $m=n-1$ (23S1 Q1a, 25S1 Q1a).
- **Fundamental period:** the smallest $T>0$. To rule out smaller periods, use a feature that occurs only once per period, such as a unique peak (25S1 Q1a).
- **Sum of sinusoids:** periodic iff $T_1/T_2=k/m$ is rational. Then $T_0=mT_1=kT_2$, or $f_0=\gcd(f_1,f_2)$ (24S1 Q1a).
- **Complex exponential** $e^{j\omega_0t}$: $T_0=2\pi/\omega_0$ (23S2 Q1a).
- **Discrete time:** $x[n]=\cos(\Omega_0n)$ is periodic iff $\frac{\Omega_0}{2\pi}=\frac mN$ is rational. In lowest terms, $N$ is the period. For a sum, take the LCM (25S2 Q3b).

### B. Energy and power (Signals p.17–22)
- $E=\int|x|^2dt$ and $P=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}|x|^2dt$. For a periodic signal, $P=\frac1{T_0}\int_{T_0}|x|^2$.
- Energy signal: $0<E<\infty$ (so $P=0$). Power signal: $0<P<\infty$ (so $E=\infty$). **Neither:** unbounded growth such as $t$ or $(2-t)u(t-3)$ (25S2 Q1a).
- **Trap:** a signal that settles to a constant $c$ only for $t>0$ has $P=c^2/2$, not $c^2$, because half the symmetric window is empty (24S1 Q1b: $P=9/2$).
- Finite duration and bounded ⇒ energy signal (23S2 Q1b: $E=58/3$). Periodic and non-zero ⇒ power signal.

### C. Singularity functions and sketching (Signals p.43–52)
- **Bookkeeping:** at each time $t_0$, a jump of size $J$ adds $J\,u(t-t_0)$, and a slope change $\Delta m$ adds $\Delta m\,r(t-t_0)$ (23S2 Q1b).
- **Evaluating an expression:** tabulate the interval-by-interval sum (24S1 Q1b, 25S2 Q1a). Note that $r(t)u(t-3)=t\,u(t-3)=r(t-3)+3u(t-3)$: a jump of 3, then a ramp.
- Always check one point per interval.

### D. Spectra, power spectrum, Parseval (Signals p.37–42; Transforms p.25, 57–61)
1. Use trig identities to turn products and powers into sums: $\sin A\cos B$, $\cos A\sin B$, $\cos^2$.
2. Write each term as $a\cos(2\pi ft+\theta)$ with $a>0$: $\sin\theta=\cos(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})$ and $-\cos\theta=\cos(\theta\pm\pi)$.
3. **Double-sided spectrum:** lines of height $a/2$ at $\pm f$, with phase $+\theta$ at $+f$ and $-\theta$ at $-f$.
4. **Power spectrum:** $|c_k|^2$ at each line. Total power is $\sum|c_k|^2$ (Parseval), which equals $\sum a^2/2$ (23S1 Q1b).

**Traps:**
- A **complex** signal such as $8e^{j10\pi t}$ gives a single line, with no mirror at $-f$ (24S1 Q1a).
- Use $j e^{j\theta}=e^{j(\theta+\pi/2)}$.
- $\cos^2(120\pi t)$ gives 120 Hz, not 240 Hz.

### E. Autocorrelation validity (Signals p.26–30; Transforms p.57–61)
Check **(1)** even, **(2)** $|R(\tau)|\le R(0)$, **(3)** $S(f)=\mathcal F\{R\}\ge0$ for all $f$ (the decisive test). Periodic parts are allowed.
- $\cos$ ⇒ $\frac12[\delta(f-f_0)+\delta(f+f_0)]\ge0$
- $e^{-\alpha|\tau|}$ ⇒ $\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha^2+(2\pi f)^2}>0$
- $\mathrm{sinc}$ ⇒ a box $\ge0$
- triangle ⇒ $\mathrm{sinc}^2\ge0$
- box ⇒ sinc, which goes negative ⇒ **invalid**
- $\sin$ ⇒ odd ⇒ **invalid**

(23S2 Q1c, 25S1 Q1b, Tutorial 1 Q5.)

### F. Sampling and Nyquist (Signals p.59–69; L0 p.12–25)
- $f_s\ge2f_{max}$; strictly $f_s>2f_{max}$ if there's a sine exactly at $f_{max}$ (25S2 Q1b(iii)).
- Aliasing: $f$ and $f+kf_s$ give the same samples.

### G. System properties (Systems p.9–22, 48; L0 p.40–50, 60–63)
- **Linear:** $a_1x_1+a_2x_2\mapsto a_1y_1+a_2y_2$.
  - Killers: an added constant ($+K$), nonlinear functions ($\sin x$, $x^2$, $|x|$).
  - Not killers: a time-varying gain $(t-1)x$, $x(2t)$, $x(t+2)$.
- **Time-invariant:** compare the response to $x(t-t_0)$ with $y(t-t_0)$.
  - Killers: an explicit $t$ multiplying anything ($g(t)x$, $(t-1)x$), time scaling $x(2t)$, time reversal $x(-t)$.
  - Not killers: $\sin(x)$, adding a constant.
  - **Counterexample trick:** feed a constant input $x=1$ (25S2 Q2a).
- **Causal:** the output uses no future input. $x(t+2)$, or $x(2t)$ for $t>0$, are non-causal. For LTI systems, causal ⇔ $h(t)=0$ for $t<0$.
- **Stable (LTI):** $\int|h|<\infty$ ⇔ (causal, rational) all poles are in the left half-plane / inside the unit circle.
- **Write-up template:** state the definition → apply it to the given system → conclude, with a numerical counterexample if it fails.

### H. Convolution (Systems p.23–46; L2 p.14–36)
- **With impulses:** $x*\delta(t-t_0)=x(t-t_0)$. Echoes add shifted, scaled copies (23S2 Q2b(i), 24S1 Q1c, 25S1 Q2b).
- **Algebraic:** use $u(\tau)u(t-\tau)$ to fix the limits $[0,t]$ (25S2 Q2b). Standard result: $e^{-at}u*e^{-bt}u=\frac{e^{-at}-e^{-bt}}{b-a}u(t)$.
- **Graphical:** flip and slide the simpler signal. Write the overlap limits as $[\max(\cdot),\min(\cdot)]$ and list the 5 cases (no overlap / entering / inside / leaving / none). The output starts at the sum of the start times and ends at the sum of the end times (23S1 Q2a).
- **Step-response trick:** write the input as steps and use L + TI, so $y=\sum c_ky_u(t-t_k)$ (25S1 Q2a). Alternatively $h=\frac{d}{dt}y_u$.
- **Properties:** commutative, distributive, associative (cascade = convolve the impulse responses).

### I. Fourier series and transform (Transforms p.10–63)
- **Pairs:**
  - $\Pi(t/T)\leftrightarrow T\,\mathrm{sinc}(fT)$, with $\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}$
  - duality: $\frac{\sin(\pi Tt)}{\pi t}\leftrightarrow\Pi(f/T)$
  - $e^{-at}u\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{a+j2\pi f}$
  - $e^{-a|t|}\leftrightarrow\frac{2a}{a^2+(2\pi f)^2}$
  - $\cos2\pi f_0t\leftrightarrow\frac12[\delta(f\mp f_0)]$
  - $\delta(t-t_0)\leftrightarrow e^{-j2\pi ft_0}$
- **Properties:**
  - time shift: multiply by $e^{-j2\pi ft_0}$
  - modulation: $e^{j2\pi f_1t}x\leftrightarrow X(f-f_1)$
  - convolution ↔ product, and product ↔ convolution
  - scaling: $x(at)\leftrightarrow\frac1{|a|}X(f/a)$
  - differentiation: $j2\pi f\,X$
  - integration: $\frac{X}{j2\pi f}+\frac12X(0)\delta(f)$
- **Modulated pulse (RF pulse):** $A\Pi(t/T)\cos2\pi f_ct\leftrightarrow\frac{AT}{2}\{\mathrm{sinc}[T(f-f_c)]+\mathrm{sinc}[T(f+f_c)]\}$ (23S2 Q2c; Transforms p.39–47).
- **Product of a pulse and $(1+\cos)$:** convolve with deltas, i.e. place shifted copies (23S1 Q2c).
- **sinc × sinc integrals:** turn them into a product of boxes (24S1 Q2b).
- **Check every FT** with $X(0)=\int x\,dt$.

### J. Frequency response and sinusoidal steady state, continuous time (Systems p.49–52; Transforms p.62; L6 p.19–24)
A stable LTI system maps $A\cos(\omega t+\theta)\mapsto A|H(j\omega)|\cos(\omega t+\theta+\angle H(j\omega))$, and DC to $H(0)\times$ DC.
- **Angles:** compute each factor's angle in the correct quadrant (23S1 Q3b(iii): $\angle(-0.5+2j)=104^\circ$, not $-76^\circ$).
- **Stability first:** for an unstable system, $H(j\omega)$ isn't a frequency response.

### K. DFT/IDFT by hand (L1 p.20–56)
- $X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$ and $x[n]=\frac1N\sum_kX[k]e^{+j2\pi kn/N}$. The course puts **no $1/N$ in analysis**.
- **4-point:** the powers are $j^{kn}\in\{1,j,-1,-j\}$. The analysis matrix rows are $[1,1,1,1]$, $[1,-j,-1,j]$, $[1,-1,1,-1]$, $[1,j,-1,-j]$; synthesis uses the conjugate matrix divided by 4.
- **Fast route:** $x[n]=\frac14\big[X_0+X_2(-1)^n+2\,\mathrm{Re}\{X_1j^n\}\big]$ when $X_3=X_1^*$ (real $x$).
- **Checks:** $\sum x=X[0]$; real $x$ ⇔ $X[N-k]=X[k]^*$.
- **The $N=5$ matrix** has entries $1\angle(-72^\circ\,kn)$. Row $N-k$ is the conjugate of row $k$ (23S1 Q4a).
- **$P$ periods in an $NP$-point DFT:** $X_{NP}[Pk]=P\,X_N[k]$ and the other bins are 0 (25S2 Q3a(iii)).

### L. DTFS ↔ DTFT (L2 p.5–13, 81–108)
- DTFS: periodic $x$, $N$ harmonics $\Omega_k=2\pi k/N$, finite sums.
- DTFT: aperiodic, absolutely summable $x$, continuous $\Omega$, $2\pi$-periodic.
- A periodic signal's DTFT is a train of impulses $2\pi a_k\delta(\Omega-2\pi k/N)$.
- To find the DTFS **by inspection**, write each term as $c\,e^{j2\pi kn/N}$ and read off $k$. A negative $k$ maps to $N+k$ (25S2 Q3b).

### M. Linear vs circular convolution (L2 p.14–80)
- Multiplying $N$-point DFTs gives **circular** convolution: anything beyond index $N-1$ wraps around to the start.
- To get linear convolution, pad both to $N\ge L_x+L_h-1$ (23S2 Q4a: $128+20-1=147$; 256 for a radix-2 FFT).

### N. Z-transform (L3)
- **Pairs:** $\gamma^nu[n]\leftrightarrow\frac{z}{z-\gamma}$ with ROC $|z|>|\gamma|$; $-\gamma^nu[-n-1]\leftrightarrow$ the same formula with $|z|<|\gamma|$; $\delta\leftrightarrow1$; $x[n-k]\leftrightarrow z^{-k}X$.
- **Inversion:** expand **$X(z)/z$** in partial fractions, then multiply back by $z$ so every term has the form $\frac{z}{z-p}$ (all papers' Q4).
- **ROC from the signal type:** right-sided ⇒ outside the outermost pole; left-sided ⇒ inside the innermost pole; two-sided ⇒ an annulus.
- **Poles and zeros:** write $H$ in positive powers of $z$ and factor. A term like $1+0.5z^{-1}$ hides a zero at $-0.5$ (and $\frac{z}{\cdot}$ hides one at $0$).

### O. Difference equations with Z (L4)
1. **$H(z)$:** transform with zero ICs. The $x$-coefficients go in the numerator (zeros) and the $y$-coefficients in the denominator (poles). Example: $y=x+0.5x[n-1]+0.1y[n-1]$ ⇒ $H=\frac{1+0.5z^{-1}}{1-0.1z^{-1}}=\frac{z+0.5}{z-0.1}$.
2. **Causal** ⇒ ROC $|z|>\max|p|$. **Stable** ⇔ the ROC contains $|z|=1$ ⇔ (for causal systems) every $|p|<1$.
3. **$h[n]$:** invert $H(z)$, e.g. $H=-5+\frac{6z}{z-0.1}$. A constant term gives a $\delta[n]$ (25S1 Q4a(v), 23S2 Q4c).
4. **Step / general response:** $Y=HX$, then partial fractions on $Y/z$. Poles of $X$ give the **steady state** (forced response); poles of $H$ give the **transient** (25S1 Q4a(iv), 24S1 Q4b).
5. **Delayed input:** solve for the undelayed input, then shift the answer (23S1 Q4b(ii)).
6. **Steady state by frequency response:** $A\cos(\Omega n+\theta)\mapsto A|H(e^{j\Omega})|\cos(\Omega n+\theta+\angle H)$. DC uses $H(1)$ and $(-1)^n$ uses $H(-1)$ (23S1 Q4b(i), 25S1 Q4a(iv), 25S2 Q3c).
7. **Always check** $y[0], y[1]$ by running the recursion. It takes 30 seconds and catches sign slips.
8. **FIR vs IIR:** no $y[n-k]$ terms (all poles at 0) means FIR, which is always stable. With feedback it's IIR.

### P. Laplace transform (L5)
- **Pairs:**
  - $e^{at}u\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{s-a}$, with $a$ possibly complex: $e^{jt}u\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{s-j}$ (25S1 Q3b)
  - $t^{m-1}e^{at}u/(m-1)!\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{(s-a)^m}$
  - $e^{\alpha t}\cos\beta t\,u\leftrightarrow\frac{s-\alpha}{(s-\alpha)^2+\beta^2}$ and $e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t\,u\leftrightarrow\frac{\beta}{(s-\alpha)^2+\beta^2}$
  - $\delta\leftrightarrow1$
- **Phase-shifted cosine:** expand $\cos(\beta t+\theta)$ first. Do **not** use the time-shift rule (24S1 Q3c).
- **ROC:** a causal signal has ROC to the right of the rightmost pole. A sum has the intersection of the ROCs.
- **Improper fractions:** if deg(num) = deg(den), divide first. The constant becomes a $\delta(t)$ (23S2 Q3b(ii)).

### Q. ODEs with Laplace (L6)
- **Unilateral transform:**
  - $\mathcal L\{y'\}=sY-y(0^-)$
  - $\mathcal L\{y''\}=s^2Y-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)$
- **Split the response:** zero-input (ICs only) + zero-state (input only) = complete response. Separately, transient (decays) + steady state (persists).
- **$h(t)$:** zero ICs and $x=\delta$, so $H(s)=Y/X$. Choose the causal ROC, because the equation is solved forward in time.
- **IVT:** $y(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sY$. **FVT:** $y(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sY$, **only if** $sY$ has every pole in the left half-plane. A simple pole of $Y$ at 0 is OK; poles on the axis, repeated poles at 0, or right-half-plane poles are not (25S2 Q4b(ii)).
- **Unstable example:** a double pole at $+1$ gives $te^t$ growth, so there is no steady state (25S1 Q3c).

### R. Relationships and "discuss" answers (L5 p.17–19, 68–70; L3 p.15–16)
- **Laplace = CTFT of $x(t)e^{-\sigma t}$**; $s=\sigma+j\omega$; the CTFT is $X(s)|_{s=j\omega}$ if the $j\omega$-axis is in the ROC.
- **Z = DTFT of $x[n]r^{-n}$**; $z=re^{j\Omega}$; the DTFT is $X(z)|_{z=e^{j\Omega}}$ if the unit circle is in the ROC.
- The two connect through $z=e^{sT}$: the left half-plane maps inside the unit circle, the $j\omega$-axis maps onto the unit circle, and the right half-plane maps outside it.
- **The same numbers mean different things:** poles near $s=0$ mean low-pass; poles near $z=-1$ mean high-pass (24S1 Q4a).

## 3. One table for all five transforms

| | CTFT | Laplace | DTFT | Z | DFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signal | $x(t)$, aperiodic | $x(t)$ | $x[n]$, aperiodic | $x[n]$ | $N$ samples, treated as periodic |

## 4. Ready-made "discuss" answers (about 6–8 marks each)

- **CTFT vs Laplace** (23S2 Q3c, 24S1 Q3a): definitions → $s=\sigma+j\omega$ → Laplace = CTFT of $xe^{-\sigma t}$ → ROC is a right half-plane for causal signals → CTFT $=X(j\omega)$ iff the axis is in the ROC → examples $e^{-t}u$ (yes) and $e^{2t}u$ (no) → poles: left half-plane decays, right half-plane grows → unilateral form handles ICs.
- **Roles of poles and zeros, s vs z** (25S2 Q4a, 24S1 Q4a): zeros block or shape; poles are modes, set the ROC and decide stability → causal ROC shapes → stability test (axis vs circle) → evaluation contour for the frequency response → geometric distances → validity needs the contour in the ROC.
- **Convolution-theorem steps** (24S1 Q2b(i)): FT both → multiply → inverse FT; it's easier because there are no case-by-case integrals, $e^{j\omega t}$ is an eigenfunction, cascades become products, and the FFT is $O(N\log N)$.
- **DFT for linear convolution** (23S2 Q4a): length $L_x+L_h-1$ → pad both → DFT, multiply, IDFT → otherwise circular wrap-around → diagram.
- **DTFT → DTFS** (25S2 Q3b): needs periodicity → $\Omega$ becomes $2\pi k/N$ → finite sums → impulses become lines → find $N$ by LCM.

## 5. Mistakes these papers punish

1. Using the wrong quadrant for an angle when the real part is negative (23S1 Q3b(iii), 25S2 Q3c).
2. Forgetting $X(z)/z$ before partial fractions, so the result isn't in $\frac{z}{z-p}$ form.
3. Treating $\cos(3t+\frac{\pi}{4})u(t)$ or $e^{j(t+3)}u(t)$ as time-shifted signals. The $u(t)$ isn't shifted, so they're not.
4. Using the FVT without the pole check, or using $H(j\omega)$ / $H(e^{j\Omega})$ on an unstable system.
5. Adding a mirror line at $-f$ for a complex exponential.
6. Taking $P=c^2$ instead of $c^2/2$ for a signal that is non-zero only for $t>0$.
7. Wrong graphical-convolution boundaries. Check: the output support runs from (start of $x$ + start of $h$) to (end of $x$ + end of $h$).
8. Mixing up $\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}$ with $\frac{\sin x}{x}$. Some paper hints print the wrong one.
9. Missing $\delta$ terms when the numerator degree equals the denominator degree ($2\delta(t)$ in 23S2 Q3b; $-5\delta[n]$ in 23S2 Q4c and 25S1 Q4a).

### Errors in the papers' own hints (don't let them throw you)
- **23S1 Q2c and 23S2 Q2c:** both say $\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}$; it must be $\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}$ for $\Pi(t/T)\leftrightarrow T\mathrm{sinc}(fT)$ to hold.
- **25S1 Q1b:** $e^{-\alpha|\tau|}\leftrightarrow\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha^2+(2\pi f)^2}$ (the hint drops the $\alpha$), and $\mathrm{sinc}(f_0\tau)\leftrightarrow\frac1{f_0}\Pi(\frac{f}{f_0})$ (the hint writes $\Pi(\frac{f}{2f_0})$). Neither changes the conclusion.
- **24S1 Q3b:** $h(t)$ is missing $u(t)$. Assume causal and say so.
- **25S1 Q4a(iv):** "state response" means steady-state response.
- **23S2 Q2c:** the text says the carrier amplitude is $A$, but the figure shows $\cos(2\pi f_ct)$. Follow the figure (it matches the slides) and state the assumption.
- **23S1 Q2a = Tutorial 3 Ex 3(f):** the tutorial's official key has the wrong case boundaries (see `Tut/Part1/Disagreements.md`).
